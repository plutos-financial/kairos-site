How Plutos keeps savings safe

Plutos makes four promises about your money. Each one is written down as a test that a computer tries to break, thousands of times, every time the code changes.

01

The promises

1Your deposit is always yours

You can withdraw everything, at any time, even if deposits are paused. Nobody else can move your savings.

2Prizes only come from interest

Every prize dollar is interest the Treasury bills earned. Deposits are never used for prizes.

3Nobody chooses the winners

Chainlink supplies the random number with a proof the blockchain checks. Plutos can't pick or change it.

4The vault holds what it owes

At every moment, the vault holds at least everyone's savings, plus prizes and fees not yet paid.

02

How they're tested

The savings contract is tested three ways. All of it runs automatically on every change to the code.

Invariant tests: try to break the promises

A program plays savers, the keeper and the team at once: deposits and withdrawals of any size, house money going in and out, long waits, weekends when the T-bill fund is slow to pay out, prize changes, draws, and draws whose random number arrives late. It runs 256 random sequences of 150 actions, and after every single action it checks:

  • Solvent: what the vault holds covers everything it owes.
  • Prizes only from interest: every prize dollar paid, waiting or locked in a draw was harvested interest, and total savings equal deposits minus withdrawals plus prizes, to the cent.
  • Every saver's ledger: each balance equals what that person put in, minus what they took out, plus what they won.
  • Entries add up: everyone's entries sum to the total the draw will use, and house money never has any.

At the end of every sequence, every saver and the house withdraw everything, the test waits for the T-bill fund to settle, and checks that each one got back every cent.

A brute-force check of every draw

The vault picks winners with a fast data structure (a sum tree, explained in how it's built). The test rebuilds each draw the slow, obvious way: it integrates every saver's balance over time, lines everyone up, and searches one by one. Across 1,000 randomized runs of four rounds each, including savers who join while a draw waits for its random number, the vault has to agree exactly: the same entries, the same totals, the same winners.

A second brute-force check does the same for the loyalty boost. Over 1,000 randomized runs of 16 rounds, it keeps every saver's deposits as dated lots, ages each dollar round by round, takes withdrawals from the newest dollars first, and requires the vault's entries and winners to match at every step.

Scenario tests

Thirty-one tests walk through specific situations: the full loop from deposit to prize to withdrawal, withdrawals during a weekend that wait for the fund to settle, the last saver leaving, withdrawals while deposits are paused, savings that can't be transferred, a draw whose random number arrives without enough gas to pay the winners in the same step, a random number that never arrives, house money that earns for the prizes but can never win, each step of the loyalty boost, and a real Chainlink VRF setup on a simulated network.

03

What the tests found

The invariant tests found one real problem, and it's fixed.

Fixed

Rounding could leave the vault a millionth of a dollar short

Buying T-bill fund tokens rounds down, by up to two millionths of a dollar per purchase. Right after a deposit was invested, the vault could briefly owe a millionth of a dollar more than it held. Harmless in practice, but it broke promise 4, and it could add up. The fix is standard: a $1 rounding reserve, put in once when the vault is set up. It never counts as interest, so it's never paid out as prizes, and it covers rounding hundreds of thousands of times over.

04

The scanner

Every contract was also run through Slither, an open-source analyzer from Trail of Bits that looks for known patterns of smart-contract bugs. After fixes it reports no high-severity findings.

  • Fixed: checks that addresses aren't empty, events whenever the owner changes a setting (so every change is on the public record), and clearer code where the scanner couldn't follow how winners were stored.
  • "Reentrancy" (reviewed, safe): every function that moves money is locked against being re-entered mid-way, and it only ever calls Plutos's own T-bill fund and USDC.
  • "Unused return values" (reviewed, intended): after each call to the fund, the vault reads its balances again rather than trusting what the call said.
  • "Strict equality" (reviewed, intended): these are checks for "is this zero?", which is exactly what's meant.
  • Low and informational: mostly that rounds are timed with the blockchain's clock, which is how they're meant to work.

05

Before real money

Tests and scanners catch a lot, but they aren't an audit. Plutos runs on a test network with test money, and won't take real deposits until all of this is done:

  • Two independent security audits, and a bug bounty.
  • Settings that need several people to sign off, with a delay before any change takes effect, plus monitoring and alerts.
  • The right legal structure, likely with a bank or credit union partner.
  • A real Treasury bill fund or bank partner in place of the test fund, and identity checks for every saver.

06

Check it yourself

The contracts and tests are public. With Foundry installed:

cd contracts
./install-deps.sh
forge test        # all 37 tests, including the invariants
forge test --match-test test_DrawCostStaysFlat -vv   # the gas table

On the test network, every deposit, draw and prize is public on Basescan, and the Draws page re-does each draw's math in your browser and checks it against what the blockchain recorded.
