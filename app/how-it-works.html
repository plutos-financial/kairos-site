How Plutos works

Plutos is prize-linked savings. You deposit from your bank, your money is kept apart from Plutos's own, the interest from all savers is pooled, and once a month it's paid out as prizes. Your deposit is never used for prizes.

01

The idea

A regular savings account pays everyone a small amount of interest. Plutos pools that interest and pays it out as prizes instead. Each month some savers win, and everyone keeps their full deposit.

The idea has a long track record. The UK government has offered Premium Bonds since 1956, and more than 22 million people hold them today. In the U.S., a 2014 federal law allows banks and credit unions to offer prize-linked savings in states that permit it, and more than 30 states do. Plutos is building a version anyone can open from their phone, and is running it now as a small, invite-only pilot.

02

Where your money goes

What happens to a $100 deposit, from start to finish:

  1. You deposit $100 from your bank.By bank transfer through Stripe, from an account in your name. It counts once the transfer settles, usually in a few business days. From then on it's 100 entries in every draw while it stays saved.
  2. It's kept apart from Plutos's money.During the pilot it sits in an account at Stripe used only for savers' money.
  3. It earns interest.The interest from all savers goes into one shared pool.
  4. The pool becomes the prizes.85% of the interest is paid out as prizes and Plutos keeps 15% to run the service. There are no fees to deposit or withdraw.
  5. Draw day.A public random number from drand picks the winners, and their prizes are added to their savings.
  6. Your $100 is still $100.Win or not, your deposit stays the same, and you're entered in next month's draw automatically.

More savers means more entries in each draw, but also a bigger prize pool. Your expected share stays about the same, and there's room for more and bigger prizes.

Only interest funds prizes. Deposits are never used to pay them.

04

How the draw works

Every dollar is an entry

Entries also count how long your money was saved during the month. $100 saved for the whole month counts the same as $200 saved for half of it, so a large deposit made the night before a draw doesn't get an unfair advantage.

Money that stays in counts for more

Each dollar's entries grow the longer it stays saved: 1x for new money, 1.5x after 3 months, 2x after 6 months and 3x after a year. Adding more never resets the boost on money that's already in. Taking money out takes your newest dollars first, so your oldest dollars keep their boost.

A public random number picks the winners

When the month ends, Plutos locks everyone's entries and publishes a fingerprint of them: a short code that would change if a single entry changed. Only then is the random number taken, from drand, a public randomness beacon run by a group of independent organizations that releases a new signed number every few seconds. Plutos uses the first one released after the fingerprint was published and checks its signature. Because the entries were fixed before the number existed, nobody, including Plutos, can influence who wins. Each prize goes to a saver in proportion to their entries.

Anyone can re-check a draw

The fingerprint and the drand number are published for every draw, so the winners can be worked out again by anyone who has them. Members can re-run their own draw in the app and see that it matches.

Prizes go straight into your savings

There's nothing to claim. If you win $25, your balance goes up by $25.

Your odds

Exact odds change whenever someone deposits or withdraws. The rule of thumb: double your savings and you double your chances, and keep them in for a year and each dollar counts three times.

05

Adding and withdrawing

Before your first deposit you confirm your identity once, with a photo of your ID and a selfie, checked by Stripe. Then you link a checking or savings account in your name by signing in to your bank. Plutos never sees your bank password.

Deposits are bank transfers through Stripe. A transfer usually takes a few business days to settle, and your balance and your entries go up when it does, not before.

You can withdraw at any time, back to the same bank account, with no fee. The app shows you exactly what a withdrawal changes before you confirm it. Bank transfers out also take a few business days to arrive.

One thing to know: interest earned on money you take out before the month's draw doesn't go into that month's prize pool. Plutos keeps it.

06

Good to know

  • Most months, most people don't win. On average, savers receive 85% of the interest their savings earn, paid as prizes. In some months you may win nothing. If you want a steady, predictable return, a high-yield savings account is the better choice.
  • Plutos is an invite-only pilot. It is open to a small group of savers while we test every part of it with real money. Join the waitlist to hear when it opens in your state.
  • Plutos isn't a bank. Your savings are not FDIC insured.
  • Bank transfers can be reversed. A bank can take a deposit back for some days after it arrives. If that happens, the deposit comes off your balance.

07

FAQ

Is this gambling?

You never risk your deposit, which is the main difference from a lottery ticket or a bet. Prize-linked savings is legal for banks and credit unions in the states that allow it, and it isn't treated as a lottery there because nobody pays to enter. We're working out exactly how Plutos fits these rules before launch.

What if I never win?

Then you haven't lost anything, but you also haven't earned interest on that money. That's the trade-off.

How much do I need to start?

$1. Every dollar you save is an entry.

Is my money in crypto?

No. Your savings are U.S. dollars, and money moves in and out by ordinary bank transfer. The demo is a separate, earlier test edition built on a blockchain test network with test money.

Who's behind Plutos?

Plutos Savings, Inc., a small team running Plutos as an invite-only pilot. Join the waitlist and we'll keep you posted.

Can I try it without an invite?

You can try the demo. It uses free test dollars, not real money, and runs on a test network, so it works differently from the pilot in how money moves and where the random number comes from. The savings, entries and prizes behave the same way.
