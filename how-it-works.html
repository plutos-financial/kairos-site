How Kairos works

The short version: you save, your money earns interest, and once a month the interest is handed out as prizes. Nobody loses their deposit. Here's the longer version, with the trade-offs.

The idea Where the money goes Where prizes come from The draw The honest part Questions

1

The idea

A normal savings account pays everyone a little bit of interest. Kairos pools that interest and pays it out as prizes instead. Each month a handful of savers win something, and everyone keeps every dollar they put in.

It isn't a new idea. Britain's government has run Premium Bonds since 1956, and more than 22 million people hold them today. In the U.S., a 2014 federal law lets banks and credit unions offer prize-linked savings in states that allow it, and more than 30 do. So far it's mostly stuck at small local credit unions. We want it to be something anyone can open from their phone in a couple of minutes.

2

Follow $100

Here's what happens to a deposit, start to finish.

  1. You deposit $100.That's 100 entries in every draw for as long as it stays saved.
  2. It's put into U.S. Treasury bills.Short-term loans to the U.S. government, one of the safest places money can sit. Some cash is kept aside so withdrawals are instant.
  3. The Treasury bills earn interest.Every saver's interest goes into one shared pot.
  4. The pot becomes the prize pool.85% goes to prizes. Kairos keeps 15% to keep the lights on. There's no fee to deposit or withdraw.
  5. Draw day.Winners are picked at random and their prizes land in their savings.
  6. Your $100 is still $100.Win or not, nothing comes out of your deposit. You're automatically in next month's draw.

3

Where the prizes come from

Only from interest. Here's a made-up but realistic month, so you can see the size of things.

Treasury bills paying 4% a year earn, per month$16,667
Kairos keeps 15%−$2,500
Prize pool this month$14,167

Split into, say, a $8,500 top prize and a few smaller ones. 4% is only an example: Treasury rates go up and down.

More savers means more entries in the hat, but it also means a bigger pool. Your fair share stays about the same, and there's room for more prizes and bigger ones.

4

How the draw picks winners

Every dollar is an entry

Entries also count how long your money was saved that month. $100 saved for the whole month counts the same as $200 saved for half of it. That way nobody can drop in a big deposit the night before a draw and leave the next morning.

A random number picks the winners

When the month ends, a random number picks winners one prize at a time. More entries means more chances. The same person can win more than one prize.

In this demo, the random number comes from the test network itself. The real version would use Chainlink VRF, a service that produces random numbers anyone can check weren't rigged, including us.

Prizes go straight into your savings

There's nothing to claim and nothing to cash in. If you win $25, your balance goes up by $25.

Your odds, without the math

We don't show exact odds, because they shift every time someone deposits or withdraws. What matters is simple: double your savings, double your chances.

5

Taking money out

Your deposit is yours, and you can take it out whenever you like. Most withdrawals are instant, because Kairos keeps a slice of everyone's money as cash instead of putting every dollar into Treasury bills. If lots of people withdraw at once, a big withdrawal might wait until some Treasury bills are sold, which usually takes about a business day. In the demo that wait is a couple of minutes.

6

The honest part

  • Most months, most people won't win. On average, savers get back 85% of what the Treasury bills earn, all of it as prizes. But averages hide a lot: plenty of people win small amounts or nothing in a given month, while a few win big. If you want a steady, predictable return, a high-yield savings account is the better pick. Kairos is for people who'd rather have a real shot at something bigger without putting their deposit at risk.
  • It isn't live yet. Before Kairos takes a single real dollar, we need the legal setup right, independent security audits, and identity checks on new customers, the same as any financial company.
  • It isn't a bank. Kairos isn't FDIC insured. The demo runs on Base Sepolia, a test network, with fake dollars that have no value.

7

Questions people ask us

Isn't this just gambling?

You never risk your deposit, which is the big difference from a lottery ticket or a bet. Prize-linked savings is legal for banks and credit unions in the states that allow it, and it isn't treated as a lottery there because nobody pays to enter. We still have to work out exactly how Kairos fits those rules before launch.

What if I never win?

Then you haven't lost anything, but you haven't earned anything on that money either. That's the trade-off, and it's why we're upfront about it.

How much do I need to start?

$10. Every dollar after that is another entry.

Why is it built on a blockchain?

So anyone can check the rules and the results. Every deposit, prize and random number is public, and nobody, including us, can quietly change who won. You don't need to know anything about crypto to use it: you sign in with your email and see dollars.
